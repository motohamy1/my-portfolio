# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

A mixed audience arriving in a first-visit, few-minutes context: recruiters and hiring managers scanning for a frontend/creative developer, freelance and contract clients evaluating whether to trust them with a build, and fellow devs/peers judging craft. All three need the same thing fast — proof this person ships interfaces worth remembering, and a clear, low-friction path to contact.

## Product Purpose

A single-page portfolio for a frontend/creative developer. It presents identity, services, skills, and selected projects, and converts attention into a hire/contact inquiry. Success = a visitor can say in under a minute who this person is, what they build, and how to reach them — while remembering the site itself as evidence of the craft being claimed.

## Brand Personality

Confident, experimental, cinematic. Three words: **bold, crafted, alive.** Voice is an expert creative developer speaking plainly — first-person, specific, no filler. The site is a studio reel, not a résumé PDF in a browser. Emotional goal: "this person operates at a level I want on my team/project."

## Anti-references

- Generic AI-portfolio slop: cream/beige minimal templates, hero-metric blocks, `01 · About` eyebrows, icon-card grids.
- Corporate SaaS polish: the "Hi, I'm ___" starter-template copy, sanitized blue-gradient trust-face.
- Gimmicky 3D-for-3D's-sake: motion that reads as screensaver chaos, janky perpetual spinners, physics toys with no payoff. If a scene doesn't say something about the work, it's cut.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the strongest project.** Every section is portfolio evidence for the claim "I build exceptional interfaces" — practice what you preach.
2. **Motion is choreography, not decoration.** Scroll-linked reveals and transitions earn their place by guiding attention through the narrative; they must degrade gracefully with reduced motion.
3. **Editorial confidence over template friendliness.** Committed dark "velvet" stage, strong typographic hierarchy, deliberate asymmetry — a studio presence, not a form letter.
4. **Show, don't tell.** Projects demonstrate themselves (real screenshots, live interactions) instead of describing themselves.
5. **One route to the yes.** Contact is the conversion; it stays reachable from every scroll position without shouting.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA: body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1 against the dark velvet backgrounds. Every 3D/GSAP/motion sequence has a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` alternative (crossfade or static state, never content gated on an unfired animation). Keyboard-operable interactions and visible focus states throughout; color is never the only signal.
